    Design your own online course

    This workshop is the course through-line: at every chapter, you apply what you learn to your own project. By the end, you won't just have reviewed concepts; you'll have built a real course, ready to publish.

    🚀

    The idea in one sentence

    Pick a topic you know well and turn it, step by step, into a coherent mini online course. Work in parallel with the chapters: each chapter of the demo course directly feeds a deliverable of your project.

    1. Context & goal

    A course isn't learned by reading: it's learned by making. The goal of this workshop is simple: apply the four stages of the course (scope, structure, produce resources, assess) to a topic of your own, then make it accessible and ready to deliver.

    No need to aim big: a narrow, concrete topic beats an ambitious program that never ships. "Make a great vinaigrette", "Read a stock chart", "Set up a VPN": a clear objective, reachable in a handful of sequences.

    2. The mission

    Your mission, from the start of the course to the end: design, write and prepare the delivery of a mini online course on the topic of your choice. You play the instructional designer: you decide the scope, write the content, and build what's needed to assess and support your learners.

    Work in iterations, chapter by chapter, rather than all at once. Here is the suggested rhythm:

    🎯After chapter 1

    Scope it: audience, prerequisites, measurable objectives, through-line. → Scoping sheet.

    🗂️After chapter 2

    Break the topic into modules and sequences. → Plan / syllabus.

    ✍️After chapter 3

    Write and illustrate one full sequence, then re-read it for accessibility. → 1 written chapter.

    🎁At the end

    Build a way to check learning (quiz and glossary), then gather everything into one coherent folder: your very own demo course.

    3. Expected deliverables

    By the end of the workshop, you submit five deliverables. Together, they form the complete skeleton of a course ready to deliver:

    📦

    Your final folder

    • A scoping sheet: target audience, prerequisites, 3 to 5 measurable learning objectives, through-line.
    • A plan / syllabus: the course tree in modules and sequences, with an estimated duration for each.
    • One fully written chapter: a complete sequence, illustrated, with a hook and a "key takeaways" box.
    • A 5-question quiz, with the correct answer marked and a short justification per question.
    • A 10-term glossary: a clear definition and, where possible, an example for each key term of the topic.
    📌

    Any format: a text document, a PDF, or even a few HTML pages modelled on this site. What matters is the consistency across the five deliverables, not the tool.

    5. Success criteria

    Your project succeeds if a colleague, browsing your folder, can understand the course without your explanations. Check every point:

    🧭Coherence

    The quiz and the written chapter actually cover the objectives in the scoping sheet.

    📏Measurable objectives

    Each objective uses an observable action verb, not a vague "understand".

    ✅Completeness

    All 5 deliverables are present: 5 quiz questions, 10 glossary terms.

    🔍Accuracy

    Every correct quiz answer is accurate and comes with a justification.

    ♿Accessibility

    Hierarchical headings, alt text planned, clear and readable language.

    💡Motivation

    A hook opens the chapter and the through-line gives meaning to the whole.

    6. Tips to succeed

    💪

    Start small, finish the project

    A finished, modest course is worth a thousand ambitious abandoned ones. Shrink the scope until you're sure you can finish; you can always enrich it later.

    🔁

    Move in step with the chapters

    Don't save the workshop for the end. As soon as a chapter is read, produce its deliverable right away: the total effort becomes painless and every concept sticks immediately.

    👥

    Get a real person to test it

    Have someone unfamiliar with the topic read your chapter and take your quiz. Their hesitations show you exactly what to clarify: it's formative assessment applied to yourself.

    ⚠️

    The trap to avoid

    Don't write the content before validating the scope and plan. Writing too early almost always means redoing everything, exactly what chapter 1 warns you against.
