    Designing an online course

    This demo course teaches you how to build a high-quality online course, while showcasing, page after page, the features of the template. Its through-line is your own course: you build it in the workshop, chapter after chapter.

    01

    The path

    Scoping, structuring, then creating the resources: the chapters follow the order in which a course is designed.

    🎯 Chapter 1 · 30 minScoping your courseA badly scoped course is hard to repair: you notice on the third session, when it is too late to start over. This chapter sets out the three decisions to make before writing a single line. It also doubles as a showcase: every engine component is exercised here at least once. 🧱 Chapter 2 · 25 minStructuring the contentScoping settled for whom and to do what. What remains is deciding in which order, and in what pieces. This is where the difference is made between a course people finish and one they abandon at chapter three. 🎬 Chapter 3 · 25 minCreating the resourcesText, audio, diagram: three formats, three different intentions. Confusing them is expensive: in production time, and in lost attention. This chapter says which to choose, and why duplication is almost always a mistake.
    02

    The through-line

    A through-line is a case, a project or a scenario that runs across the whole course. This demo course's through-line is yours: you build a course while reading a course about building one.

    In EADL, the through-line can be an application that learners design chapter after chapter: every architecture notion applies to a real decision.

    In ERIS, the through-line can be the infrastructure of a fictional company, which learners secure chapter after chapter: every notion applies to a device or a rule.

    03

    What you produce

    By the end of the workshop, you submit five deliverables. Together, they form the complete skeleton of a course ready to deliver.

    1. A scoping sheet: the target audience, the prerequisites, the measurable objectives, the through-line.
    2. A plan: the modules and sequences of the course, with their estimated duration.
    3. One fully written chapter, illustrated, with a hook and a "key takeaways" box.
    4. A quiz, with the correct answer and a short justification for each question.
    5. A glossary: a clear definition and, where possible, an example for each term.

    French and English across the site, Arabic for chapter 1, right to left. Switching language keeps your reading position.

    🎓Two programs

    A course shared by EADL and ERIS: choose your program, and the examples specific to the other one fade away.

    An audio capsule at the top of each chapter, with its transcript, interactive diagrams and a podcast player.

    Terms highlighted in the text, hover tooltip with a definition and an example.
